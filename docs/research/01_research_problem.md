# 01 — Research Problem

## Digital access depends on infrastructure

Audiovisual heritage is increasingly mediated by digital infrastructures. Public access no longer depends only on the physical location of an archive or on the existence of preserved materials. It also depends on software, metadata, interfaces, platforms, rights information, interoperability and institutional access procedures.

As a result, audiovisual memory may be preserved but remain digitally invisible, technically unstable or administratively restricted.

## The limits of static catalogues

Many initiatives describe collections, institutions or available records at a specific moment. These resources are valuable, but static descriptions cannot adequately answer longitudinal questions such as:

- Did an archive remove a public search route?
- Did access begin to require registration or a formal request?
- Was an API introduced, changed or discontinued?
- Did the institution adopt a new metadata or interoperability standard?
- Did a previously visible collection disappear from the public surface?
- Are external commercial platforms becoming more central to access?

Answering such questions requires repeated and comparable observations, not isolated collection events.

## The scientific gap

The project addresses a gap between three types of existing work:

1. **Collection catalogues**, which describe audiovisual objects but may not analyse the infrastructures through which they are accessed.
2. **Technical audits**, which may identify technologies at one moment but often do not preserve longitudinal, curatorial and methodological context.
3. **Digital observatories**, which may publish indicators without exposing the complete path from source evidence to analytical result.

Memória Audiovisual em Rede integrates these concerns into a provenance-first and longitudinal research infrastructure.

## Central research problem

The central problem can be expressed as follows:

> Under which infrastructural, institutional, technical and cultural conditions do audiovisual collections become visible, invisible, restricted or unstable in digital environments?

This problem rejects a simple equivalence between institutional existence and public digital access. It also rejects the assumption that being online necessarily means being discoverable, reusable or stable.

## Analytical dimensions

The infrastructure currently observes several connected dimensions:

### Visibility

Whether audiovisual records, catalogues and access routes can be found on public digital surfaces.

### Accessibility

Whether eligible archives can be consulted without registration, authentication, payment or formal request.

### Interoperability

Whether public evidence indicates standards and services that support exchange, harvesting or reuse of metadata and digital objects.

### Metadata practices

Whether structured descriptive formats and semantic-web mechanisms are publicly observable.

### Technological dependency

Whether archives rely on external platforms, providers or technical ecosystems that shape access and continuity.

### Artificial intelligence

Whether institutions publicly document the use of automated transcription, recognition, classification or other AI-assisted processes.

### Longitudinal stability

Whether routes, resources and detected features persist, change, disappear or become recurrently unavailable.

## Public and commercial infrastructures

The research corpus is not composed of every platform that hosts images or video. Commercial image banks and paid stock repositories may be relevant to the broader digital ecosystem, but they do not represent the same institutional and public-access problem as eligible audiovisual archives.

They are therefore documented in the discovery layer and excluded from the scientific corpus when the eligibility policy determines that their primary model is commercial access. This avoids distorting public-access indicators while preserving methodological transparency.

## Why provenance matters

A longitudinal claim is credible only when the researcher can reconstruct:

- the original source;
- the acquisition method;
- the observation date;
- the transformation process;
- the evidence supporting the classification;
- any human-review decision;
- the methodology version used in the calculation.

For this reason, provenance is not an auxiliary feature. It is a core part of the scientific design.

## Expected contribution

The project aims to contribute:

- a reusable methodology for longitudinal digital-infrastructure observation;
- an empirical record of the changing public infrastructures of audiovisual archives;
- transparent and versioned indicators;
- research datasets that preserve uncertainty and exclusion decisions;
- a bridge between audiovisual heritage studies, digital methods and infrastructure research.

The following chapters will describe the scientific framework and methodological implementation used to address this problem.
