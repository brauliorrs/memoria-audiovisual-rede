# 00 — Introduction

## Project overview

**Memória Audiovisual em Rede** is an open scientific research infrastructure for the longitudinal observation of digital infrastructures in audiovisual archives.

Its current empirical domain is audiovisual heritage, but its central contribution is methodological: a reusable framework for observing how institutional digital infrastructures change over time while preserving evidence, provenance, uncertainty, review and analytical history.

## Why audiovisual archives?

Audiovisual archives provide a particularly relevant empirical field because public access to their collections depends on multiple technical and institutional layers:

- cataloguing systems;
- public websites and search interfaces;
- metadata standards;
- streaming and delivery platforms;
- rights and access policies;
- interoperability protocols;
- authentication and formal-request procedures;
- external providers and platform dependencies;
- preservation and migration decisions.

A collection may exist physically while remaining difficult to discover or access digitally. Conversely, an institution may publish extensive institutional content without making its archival holdings directly available. The project is designed to preserve these distinctions.

## From inventory to observation

The infrastructure does not treat the Web as a source from which a definitive inventory can simply be extracted. Public digital surfaces are partial, unstable and shaped by institutional choices.

The project therefore records:

- what was observed;
- where it was observed;
- how it was obtained;
- when it was obtained;
- which transformation produced the analytical record;
- whether a human reviewed it;
- how it differs from previous observations.

This allows the platform to study not only presence, but also absence, restriction, instability and change.

## Research object and reusable method

The immediate research object is the digital infrastructure of audiovisual archives.

The reusable methodological object is a longitudinal observation framework based on:

1. discovery;
2. classification;
3. corpus eligibility;
4. structured observation;
5. provenance;
6. periodic snapshots;
7. change detection;
8. human review;
9. versioned publication;
10. reproducible analytics.

This separation is essential. It allows the project to remain empirically grounded while making its architecture potentially adaptable to museums, libraries, public repositories and other institutional digital services.

## Intended users

The infrastructure is designed for:

- researchers in communication, digital humanities and infrastructure studies;
- audiovisual heritage and archival researchers;
- public institutions and memory organisations;
- students learning computational and longitudinal research methods;
- collaborators developing comparative or cross-country studies;
- researchers interested in adapting the methodology to other domains.

## Scope and caution

The platform observes publicly available evidence. It does not claim privileged access to internal systems, contracts or technical architectures unless those facts are supported by public documents or reviewed evidence.

A detected signal is not automatically equivalent to verified institutional adoption. Absence of detection is not automatically equivalent to absence of a technology. These limitations are part of the data model rather than footnotes added after analysis.

## Current stage

The core architecture is implemented. The project is currently validating its detectors, corpus decisions, longitudinal workflow and analytical indicators against real-world cases.
