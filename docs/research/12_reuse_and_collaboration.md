# Reuse and Research Collaboration

**Memória Audiovisual em Rede** is empirically focused on audiovisual archives, but its longitudinal observation framework was designed to support adaptation to other institutional domains.

This chapter explains what is reusable, what remains domain-specific and how research collaboration should be approached.

## Reusable methodological components

The following components are potentially transferable:

- discovery and classification registries;
- explicit corpus eligibility decisions;
- evidence and provenance records;
- periodic snapshots;
- longitudinal comparison;
- event triage;
- human review and decision ledgers;
- versioned publication views;
- analytical registries;
- methodology versioning;
- append-only indicator history;
- integrity manifests and checksums.

## Domain-specific components

The following elements cannot be transferred without adaptation and validation:

- the definition of an eligible audiovisual archive;
- archive-specific access categories;
- metadata and interoperability patterns selected for observation;
- aliases used by technical detectors;
- composite-index components and weights;
- interpretation of public visibility;
- exclusion rules for commercial image and video banks.

Reusing the framework does not justify reusing domain-specific assumptions unchanged.

## Possible collaboration formats

Potential forms of collaboration include:

- methodological exchange;
- comparative pilot studies;
- detector validation;
- adaptation to another institutional domain;
- joint corpus construction;
- indicator co-design;
- reproducibility review;
- research software contributions;
- co-authored methodological or empirical publications.

Any collaboration should define the research question, unit of analysis, data responsibilities, validation procedure, attribution and publication conditions before operational collection begins.

## Adaptation protocol

A new domain should proceed through the following sequence:

```text
Research question
        ↓
Unit of analysis
        ↓
Discovery taxonomy
        ↓
Eligibility policy
        ↓
Observation parameters
        ↓
Evidence requirements
        ↓
Validation sample
        ↓
Methodology version
        ↓
Controlled pilot
```

Only after the pilot has been manually reviewed should large-scale or recurring observation be considered.

## Scientific safeguards

An adapted deployment must:

- preserve the distinction between observation and institutional fact;
- document inaccessible or non-assessable cases;
- avoid treating missing signals as negative evidence by default;
- version every methodological change;
- preserve historical observations rather than overwrite them;
- report denominators, exclusions and coverage limitations;
- avoid presenting exploratory indicators as validated measures.

## Relationship to external projects

The infrastructure may be methodologically relevant to research on public digital services, institutional platforms, technology providers, data infrastructures, AI adoption and digital governance.

Such relevance must be framed as potential methodological transfer. The current implementation does not claim to analyse those domains unless a dedicated corpus, schema, validation process and methodology have been established.

## Contribution pathway

External contributors should begin by identifying one bounded contribution, such as:

- reviewing a detector;
- documenting a standard;
- validating a small corpus sample;
- proposing a methodological clarification;
- testing reproducibility;
- translating research documentation.

Large architectural changes should be discussed before implementation so that provenance, compatibility and longitudinal continuity are preserved.
