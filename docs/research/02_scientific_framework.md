# 02 — Scientific Framework

## Purpose

Memória Audiovisual em Rede is grounded in the study of digital infrastructures as socio-technical arrangements rather than isolated software products. The project treats audiovisual archives as institutional environments shaped by technologies, standards, organisational decisions, access policies and external dependencies.

The scientific framework connects six complementary dimensions.

## Digital infrastructures

Digital infrastructures are understood as layered and evolving arrangements composed of platforms, protocols, software, metadata models, hosting environments, service providers and institutional practices. Their effects are often distributed across systems and therefore cannot be reduced to a single website or application.

The platform observes publicly detectable evidence of these arrangements while preserving the distinction between technical detection and institutional confirmation.

## Audiovisual heritage

Audiovisual archives are not analysed only as repositories of content. They are examined as institutions that organise, describe, preserve and circulate memory through digital systems.

The research therefore considers both the existence of collections and the conditions under which they become visible, searchable, interoperable, accessible or restricted.

## Accessibility and public circulation

Access is treated as a separate analytical dimension. Technical sophistication does not necessarily imply public accessibility.

An archive may adopt advanced standards while still requiring registration, authentication or formal requests. Conversely, an archive may provide direct public consultation without advanced interoperability.

The Audiovisual Archive Access Index preserves this distinction by measuring the proportion of eligible archives accessible without registration, payment or formal request.

## Metadata and interoperability

Metadata standards and interoperability protocols are observed because they influence discovery, reuse, exchange and aggregation.

The current framework includes APIs, IIIF, OAI-PMH, Dublin Core, Schema.org and JSON-LD. Their presence is interpreted as evidence of specific technical capabilities, not as proof of complete compliance, metadata quality or unrestricted access.

## Digital preservation and longitudinal change

Preservation is approached not only as the survival of files but also as the persistence of digital access routes, technical evidence, metadata practices and institutional visibility.

Periodic snapshots make it possible to document emergence, disappearance, migration, restriction and technological replacement over time.

## Artificial intelligence and automation

AI-related signals are treated cautiously. Publicly detectable references to machine learning, automated transcription, speech recognition, computer vision or automated classification are recorded as evidence requiring interpretation and, when necessary, human review.

The platform does not infer institutional AI use solely from generic technical traces.

## Computational observation

The project combines automated collection with methodological controls:

- explicit provenance;
- versioned schemas;
- deterministic identifiers;
- human review for sensitive claims;
- longitudinal snapshots;
- reproducible analytics;
- transparent exclusions.

Computational methods extend the scale and consistency of observation, but they do not eliminate the need for curatorial judgement.

## Reusable methodological contribution

Audiovisual archives are the current empirical domain, but the principal methodological contribution is broader: a reusable longitudinal framework for observing institutional digital infrastructures.

The same architecture may support future studies of museums, libraries, governmental repositories, public digital services and other institutional ecosystems, provided that each domain defines its own corpus policy, variables and validation rules.

## Analytical principle

The framework follows a central rule:

> Observable technical evidence supports documented claims only within the limits of the source, method, date and review status attached to that evidence.

This principle connects the scientific framework to the provenance, review and publication architecture described in the following chapters.
