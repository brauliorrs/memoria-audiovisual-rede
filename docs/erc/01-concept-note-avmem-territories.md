# Concept Note

## Working Title

**AVMEM-TERRITORIES - Algorithmic Territories of Audiovisual Memory**

**Subtitle:** Visibility, Infrastructure and Digital Extinction in European Audiovisual Archives

## ERC Positioning

**Likely scheme:** ERC Starting Grant, next available cycle after ERC-2027-STG.

**Primary panel, provisional:** SH3 - The Social World and Its Interactions.

**Secondary panel, provisional:** SH8 - Arts, Cultures and Societies.

The proposal should be framed as frontier research in communication, media studies and digital heritage, not as a cultural portal or digitisation project.

## Core Problem

Audiovisual memory is increasingly mediated by digital infrastructures that decide what can be found, linked, described, reused and publicly accessed. Yet the field still lacks a comparative and longitudinal explanation of the conditions under which audiovisual archives become visible, invisible or digitally extinct.

Existing research has examined digitisation, heritage policy, archives, platforms and access. What remains insufficiently understood is the mechanism by which technical interoperability, institutional governance, language, licensing, metadata quality and platform dependency combine to produce a new cultural geography of audiovisual memory.

## Main Research Question

Under which infrastructural, institutional and algorithmic conditions do audiovisual archives become visible, invisible or digitally extinct, and how do these processes reorganise the cultural geography of audiovisual memory?

## Provisional Hypothesis

The digital circulation of audiovisual memory is not primarily determined by the physical existence or size of collections. It is shaped by a combination of technical interoperability, institutional governance, language, licensing regimes, metadata practices and dependence on digital infrastructures. This combination produces a new unequal geography of memory, in which some archives become globally visible while others enter measurable processes of invisibility or digital extinction.

## Breakthrough Claim

The project will move audiovisual heritage studies beyond the opposition between preservation and access by developing a theory of **digital audiovisual visibility**: a model explaining how memory becomes public, searchable, restricted or absent through infrastructural conditions.

The expected breakthrough is not a larger map of audiovisual archives. It is an explanatory framework and empirical method for detecting, comparing and theorising the loss, migration and uneven visibility of audiovisual memory across digital infrastructures.

## Why The Platform Matters

The Memoria Audiovisual em Rede platform is preliminary evidence that the phenomenon can be observed. It already demonstrates that:

- relevant sources and aggregators exist;
- collection records can be gathered and compared;
- public, restricted and unstable access routes can be distinguished;
- metadata incompleteness and route instability are themselves analyzable evidence;
- longitudinal monitoring can capture disappearance, reappearance and changing regimes of access.

In ERC terms, the platform should be described as a research instrument that makes a previously diffuse problem measurable.

## Research Objectives

1. Build a comparative typology of digital visibility regimes in European audiovisual archives.
2. Identify the infrastructural, institutional and linguistic factors that increase or reduce detectability.
3. Measure longitudinal changes in public availability, restriction and disappearance of audiovisual records.
4. Audit the role of aggregators, search mechanisms and platform dependencies in distributing visibility.
5. Develop a Digital Audiovisual Visibility Index.
6. Formulate a theory of digital audiovisual extinction and its consequences for cultural memory.

## Provisional Work Packages

**WP1 - Conceptual Framework and State of the Art**

Define digital audiovisual visibility, invisibility and extinction. Position the project against media studies, archive studies, platform studies, digital heritage and infrastructure studies.

**WP2 - European Corpus and Data Architecture**

Define the European institutional universe, inclusion and exclusion criteria, data schema, metadata model, collection protocols and reproducibility standards.

**WP3 - Longitudinal Visibility Observatory**

Run annual or semi-annual observations of visibility, access status, metadata completeness, route stability and institutional presence across selected archives and aggregators.

**WP4 - Comparative and Statistical Analysis**

Test relationships between country, language, institution type, metadata standards, licensing, aggregation and visibility outcomes.

**WP5 - Algorithmic Findability Audits**

Examine how records are surfaced or suppressed through aggregators, public search interfaces and platform search mechanisms.

**WP6 - Qualitative Case Studies**

Conduct interviews and case studies with archives, cinemateques, aggregators and institutions representing extreme or theoretically important cases.

**WP7 - Theory, Index and Open Science Outputs**

Deliver the Digital Audiovisual Visibility Index, reproducible datasets where legally possible, methods documentation, articles and a synthetic theory of digital audiovisual extinction.

## Step 1 Story

The first five pages must not spend their energy explaining the platform. They must persuade the panel that the project addresses a frontier question:

Digital infrastructures are not neutral channels for audiovisual memory. They actively reorganise which cultural pasts become visible, which remain territorially or linguistically marginal, and which enter processes of measurable digital extinction.
